the matchbox

Image: matchbox with drawer pulled out

Wooden. Pocket-sized. Slide-out drawer.

The insert sits inside the drawer. When the balm runs out, slide the insert out and slide a new one in. The matchbox stays. Nothing about it is disposable.

Each lid carries the flavour graphic, drawn in the spirit of the small mid-century printed objects that did more with one ink than most brands do with six.

four ingredients

Honey, beeswax, coconut, jojoba. That’s the list.

No fillers, no fragrance compounds, no actives stacked for a marketing claim. Four ingredients, all of them obvious in the hand.

01 NZ honey

From beekeepers we know by name. Varietal changes with the flavour.

02 Beeswax

Gives the balm its hold. The reason it doesn’t slip.

03 Coconut oil

Softens without going greasy. Carries the flavour cleanly.

04 Jojoba oil

Finishes the texture. The polish at the end.

sustainability

Image: NZ native bush landscape

Made to keep. Built to refill.

The wooden matchbox is designed to last decades. Inserts ship in a small card envelope with no plastic. The wood is sustainably-sourced NZ pine; the beekeepers we work with manage their hives without antibiotics.

Our approach

how refills work

Made to keep.

Buy once. Refill the insert. The matchbox stays — and gets better with handling.

01 · Buy First insert installed

The matchbox arrives with its first insert ready to use. Open the drawer, twist up the balm, get on with your day.

02 · Use About three months

An insert lasts roughly a season for daily users. The balm doesn’t dry out — sealed in its own little tin inside the wood.

03 · Refill Slide out, slide in

Empty insert slides out. New insert slides in. Same matchbox, new balm. Eight dollars, ships in a card envelope.
