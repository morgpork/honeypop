the balm

Pear skin and young coconut — read it off your lip at once.

Goes on like water, sets like wax. The pear is the top note — skin, not syrup — and the coconut sits behind it, cool and flat, the way a fresh coconut tastes before anyone does anything to it. Neither one shouts.

The Rewarewa honey is the low line underneath — copper, a little caramel, quiet. You smell it more than you taste it. Wears soft. Doesn’t leave a film. By the time the balm’s gone from your mouth the pear is the last thing still there.

the ingredients

Four ingredients. That’s the list.

No fillers, no fragrance compounds, no actives stacked for a marketing claim. Four things, all of them obvious in the hand.

01 Rewarewa honey

From West Coast beekeepers. Rewarewa is the NZ honeysuckle — copper-toned honey with a caramel note under the sweet.

02 Beeswax

NZ beeswax, filtered once. Gives the balm its hold — the reason it doesn’t slip off.

03 Coconut oil

Softens without going greasy. Carries the pear and the coconut-flesh note cleanly.

04 Jojoba oil

Finishes the texture. The polish at the end — and the reason the balm wears soft, not waxy.

the lid

Sage-green ink on cream stock. One pear, mostly implied.

The Coconut & Pear lid borrows from 1970s Hokkaido produce labels — sage-green ink printed on uncoated cream stock, warm enough to read as paper rather than white. One colour, one subject, no ornament.

The pear isn’t drawn so much as suggested — its silhouette cut out of the green field by the type and a thin horizon line. Display letterforms hand-cut in the tradition of small Japanese print shops, uneven on purpose, the kind of drawing that doesn’t pretend to be a typeface.
