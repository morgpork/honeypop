the balm

The ripe one.

Mango is the warmest flavour in the range and the loudest on the lip. The first read is fruit — sun-weight, stone-fruit round, the kind of ripeness you get from a mango eaten standing over the sink. Underneath, the Kāmahi honey pulls the sweetness into something creamier and keeps it from tipping into juice.

The texture carries a little more body than Vanilla or Coconut & Pear — the mango seed oil is a heavier cold-press, and it lays the flavour down slowly rather than letting it flash off. Goes on soft, settles warm, reads as fruit at arm’s length and as honey up close.

the ingredients

Four, named.

Same four ingredients as every Honey Pop balm. Cold-pressed mango seed oil carries the flavour without sitting on top of the four-ingredient base — the actual pip of the fruit, milled and pressed. No synthetics, no fragrance compounds pretending to be mango.

01 Kāmahi honey

From Westland beekeepers working the Kāmahi forest. Pale, creamy, thick — the hardwood-sourced honey most exported out of NZ.

02 Beeswax

NZ beeswax, filtered once. The reason the balm holds its shape through a warm afternoon.

03 Coconut oil

Organic, unrefined. Softens the stack and lets the mango carry.

04 Jojoba oil

Cold-pressed. The finishing oil — the one that makes the balm sit smooth instead of slick.

the lid

One orange ink, one piece of fruit.

The Mango lid riffs on the 1950s Yokohama matchbox flora series — the small colour labels printed in saturated single inks for tea rooms and dry-goods shops on that side of the harbour. A fruit silhouette lives inside the positive shape rather than being drawn on top of it, woodcut-style, so the mango reads as mass and weight rather than outline. Hand-lettered ‘Mango’ mark sits across the middle, ink flat, no gradient, no shine.

It’s the most playful lid in the range without tipping into juvenile. The reference works because the old flora prints did the same trick — a fruit or flower pressed into one bold colour, nothing cute, nothing apologetic. The orange is the brightest in our palette; the lid earns it by being the simplest.
