the balm

Cream, not candy.

Vanilla is the quiet one in the range. The flavour sits low and warm — cream notes pulled straight out of the clover honey, a whisper of real vanilla on top, nothing sweet enough to read as dessert. It’s the flavour you reach for when you don’t want a flavour at all.

The formula softens a shade more than the Beeswax Original. A touch more coconut oil carries the vanilla cleanly and gives the balm a slightly looser pickup on the tube — it goes on light and stays put. Clover honey is the reason the finish reads as cream rather than syrup.

the ingredients

Four, named.

Same four ingredients as every Honey Pop balm. What changes per flavour is where the honey comes from and the oil pressed in with it.

01 Clover honey

From beekeepers on the Canterbury Plains. The varietal that gives vanilla its cream.

02 Beeswax

NZ beeswax, filtered once. The reason the balm holds its shape on a hot bench.

03 Coconut oil

Organic, unrefined. Carries the vanilla through without turning it sweet.

04 Jojoba oil

Cold-pressed. The last oil in — finishes the texture so it sits smooth.

the lid

A cream wordmark on pomace red.

The Vanilla lid takes its cue from 1960s Yokohama tea-packet labels — the kind you still find in the back of an old chemist’s drawer. Warm cream lockup sitting on a deep pomace red, a hand-drawn script reading “Vanilla” across the middle, flat ink, no gradients, no shine. The wood around the print is the frame.

We chose the reference because vanilla is the flavour most often turned into a cliché — pale wrappers, gold foil, a little curl of a bean. None of that tells you anything. The tea-packet tradition is the opposite: graphic confidence on a small surface, one colour doing the work of six, a label that earns its place by being read at arm’s length. The lid is the argument before the balm is the proof.
