the balm

A ripe pomegranate, split open.

Pomegranate smells like the fruit before it tastes like anything — the sharp, seedy snap you get from breaking one apart at the kitchen bench. Bright, a little tart, with a dry edge that keeps it honest. No candy, no syrup, no confected red.

Underneath, the Tāwari honey does the holding. Darker and more resinous than clover, it gives the balm a grown-up backbone so the fruit sits on top rather than drifts off. Goes on smooth, sets in a breath, leaves the lip with colour but no stickiness.

the ingredients

Four things, named on the label.

Same four ingredients as every Honey Pop flavour. What changes is the honey. For Pomegranate we use Tāwari — a North Island native, flowering late summer, dark amber with a green undertow and a sharper bite than pasture honey. It suits the fruit.

01 Tāwari honey

From Northland beekeepers working native stands. Dark amber, faintly green, sharper than clover — built for fruit flavours.

02 Beeswax

Gives the balm its hold. The reason it doesn’t slip off in the sun.

03 Coconut oil

Softens without going greasy. Carries the pomegranate cleanly, no muddiness.

04 Jojoba oil

Finishes the texture. Polishes the edge off the wax so the balm reads silk, not candle.

One ink. Woodblock feeling. A word worth printing.

The Pomegranate lid borrows from 1950s Tokyo travel posters — the kind printed in a single saturated red, woodblock-cut in feel, where the graphic was one strong mark and nothing else. No fruit illustration. No garnish. Just the word “Pomegranate” hand-drawn on a paper-white knockout panel, set against a full field of arterial red.

It’s an argument for trusting one good mark over six ordinary ones. The mid-century designers who printed matchboxes, tea packets, and travel bills had one ink and a tight budget. They made the graphic carry it.
