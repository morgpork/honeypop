the balm

The honey one.

Beeswax is the hero. It’s the original — the formula with nothing added on top. Warm honey on the nose, a resinous depth from the Mānuka, and the faint wax-and-comb smell you get near a working hive on a warm afternoon. No citrus, no vanilla, no sweet top-note. Just the four ingredients, in their own clothes.

On the lips it goes on soft, settles firm, and stays put. The balm is a little darker in the tin than the others — that’s the Mānuka doing its work. Of the six, this is the one that tastes the most like honey, because it’s the only one where honey is the flavour.

the ingredients

Four, and the honey is the point.

Every Honey Pop balm is the same four ingredients. In Beeswax, the honey is doing the flavouring — so the honey matters more here than anywhere else in the range.

01 Mānuka honey

Single-varietal, East Cape. Dark, resinous, intensely flavoured. The honey New Zealand is known for — carrying the whole balm on its own.

02 Beeswax

Gives the balm its hold. The reason it doesn’t slip. Also the reason this flavour’s called what it’s called.

03 Coconut oil

Softens without going greasy. Carries the honey cleanly, steps out of its way.

04 Jojoba oil

Finishes the texture. The polish at the end.

the lid

A 1920s apothecary tin.

The original flavour gets the most original-looking lid. One ink — burnt amber — on uncoated kraft stock. An all-caps display serif spelling out BEESWAX. A batch number, a weight, and nothing else.

We took the cue from apothecary tins of the 1920s, back when a shop sold its balm out of a metal drum and printed the lid itself on the corner press. Utilitarian-handsome. No decoration. The hierarchy does the work — the name is the loudest thing, and the rest stays quiet.
