the balm

Citrus on warm gold.

Honey & Grapefruit is the loudest flavour in the range and the one we reach for most. The grapefruit hits first — pithy, slightly bitter, the smell of the fruit still on your hands after you’ve peeled it — and then the Pōhutukawa honey lands underneath and holds it there. Warm, round, faintly saline. Nothing perfumed, nothing sweet.

On the lip it reads a shade sharper than the rest of the range. A little more grapefruit oil than we’d usually pour, balanced out by the coconut oil carrying it cleanly. The balm goes on glossy, settles matte, and leaves a low citrus warmth for about an hour. It’s the one that wakes up a dry afternoon.

the ingredients

Four, named.

Same four ingredients as every Honey Pop balm. Cold-pressed Northland grapefruit oil carries the flavour without sitting on top of the four-ingredient base — no fragrance compound, no carrier, the oil is the flavour.

01 Pōhutukawa honey

From hives sat among the coastal Pōhutukawa on the Coromandel. Light gold, round, with a sea-salt trace from the flowering cliffs.

02 Beeswax

NZ beeswax, filtered once. The reason the balm holds its shape on a hot bench.

03 Coconut oil

Organic, unrefined. Carries the grapefruit without softening its edge.

04 Jojoba oil

Cold-pressed. The last oil in — finishes the texture so it sits smooth.

Citrus orange, one word, a thin line.

The Honey & Grapefruit lid takes its cue from 1960s Shiseido cosmetic prints — the posters and packet labels Shin Matsunaga and his peers were drawing at the Ginza studios. A flat panel of citrus red-orange, borderless. One display word centred on the field, set in a geometric lockup that feels engineered rather than drawn. A single thin meta line sits under it — varietal, volume, origin — nothing else. The Honey Pop wordmark drops out of a small knockout-paper block at the top corner, the way Shiseido used to float the maison over the colour.

We chose the reference because mid-century Japanese cosmetic print did the thing citrus packaging almost never does: it trusted one colour and one word to carry a whole product. No illustrated fruit, no halftone gradient, no curl of peel. The orange is the grapefruit. The word is the flavour. The thin line is the receipt. Reading the lid at arm’s length is reading the product — which is the point of the matchbox format in the first place.
