Ingredients.

Four ingredients sit in every Honey Pop matchbox, across every flavour. On top of those: six flavour oils, each paired with one of six NZ honey varietals we source region by region.

core

The four.

Every Honey Pop matchbox uses these four, in every flavour.

NZ honey Beeswax Coconut oil Jojoba oil

NZ honey

The humectant — the active soothing ingredient that pulls moisture into dry skin. The varietal changes with the flavour, and that shift is where the character comes from.

Beeswax

Structural. Gives the balm its hold, its slow melt, and the reason it doesn’t slide off in warm pockets.

Coconut oil

Softens the blend without going greasy, and carries the flavour oil cleanly across the lip.

Jojoba oil

The last addition in the pour. Polishes the texture at the end — what turns a waxy balm into something that glides.

flavour oils

Six flavour directions.

One flavour oil per matchbox, paired with the honey varietal that suits it. One of the six carries no flavour oil at all — the honey stands alone.

Vanilla bean Pomegranate Coconut & Pear (No oil — Beeswax) Honey & Grapefruit Mango seed

Vanilla bean

Madagascar-sourced bean, cold-extracted into the oil rather than synthesised. Paired with Clover honey for the mellow, almost biscuit-sweet profile.

Pomegranate

Cold-pressed pomegranate fruit concentrate — sharp, tart, a little tannic. Paired with Tāwari honey, which has its own sharper edge.

Coconut & Pear

The coconut comes from the oil already in the base; pear extract is cold-pressed on top. Paired with Rewarewa for caramel-copper depth under the fresh fruit.

Beeswax Original

No added flavour oil. The Mānuka honey carries the whole profile — dark, herbal, unmistakably itself.

Honey & Grapefruit

Cold-pressed grapefruit oil from Northland citrus — bright, slightly bitter, warm. Paired with Pōhutukawa honey and its faint coastal salt.

Mango seed

Cold-pressed mango seed oil — a byproduct of local mango processing that would otherwise go to waste. Paired with Kāmahi honey and its dense, creamy base.

nz honey

Six varietals, six regions.

The varietal is what shifts most between flavours. Each one comes from a different part of the country and a different flowering plant — and each one reads differently on the lip.

Mānuka — East Cape Clover — Canterbury Kāmahi — Westland Pōhutukawa — Coromandel Tāwari — Northland Rewarewa — West Coast

Mānuka — East Cape

Dark, intensely flavoured, the iconic NZ honey. Harvested late summer on the East Cape. Pairs with the no-flavour Beeswax Original, where it sits front and centre.

Clover — Canterbury

Pale gold, mellow, the classic table honey — Canterbury plains, early summer pour. Pairs with Vanilla, and lets the bean do the talking.

Kāmahi — Westland

Creamy and dense, with a slight caramel note from a West Coast hardwood. Harvested midsummer. Pairs with Mango seed oil.

Pōhutukawa — Coromandel

Light gold with a faint salt edge from the coastal flowering. Christmas-window harvest. Pairs with Honey & Grapefruit.

Tāwari — Northland

A sharper edge and an amber-green tone — a North Island native that flowers briefly in early summer. Pairs with Pomegranate.

Rewarewa — West Coast

Copper-toned, caramel-forward, from the NZ honeysuckle. Harvested in the short spring window. Pairs with Coconut & Pear.
