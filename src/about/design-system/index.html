design system

Six flavours, one lid system.

Every Honey Pop matchbox lid follows the same composition. Three flat horizontal bands — deep, dominant, light. Brand mark top. Flavour name centre. Honey varietal bottom. Only the palette changes.

The grammar is borrowed from Prada Mode’s poster system, which itself descends from the Japanese matchbox label tradition of 1950s–70s Tokyo. Borrowing it back at matchbox scale closes the loop.

the rules

Five lid principles.

  1. One subject, one dominant colour, one wordmark. No secondary illustration, no subtitle.
  2. Flat colour only. One or two inks plus a knockout. No gradient, no photograph, no faux-depth. Risograph-legal.
  3. Warm-saturated palette, never pastel. The 1950s–70s Japanese palette was saturated by default. Pastel reads as Western craft market.
  4. Honey Pop wordmark in a locked position across all flavours. Top-left of the deep band, every time.
  5. Flavour mark is hand-drawn, not typeset. In these mockups it’s typeset for system preview; the production lid gets a custom-drawn wordmark per flavour.

where this comes from

Influences.

Three lineages converge on the matchbox lid. Full catalogue in visual-language-teardown.md.

  • Japanese matchbox labels

    1920s–70s Tokyo printing. Flat saturated ink, hand-lettered wordmark, single subject per 35×55mm frame.

  • Prada Mode posters

    Identity by 2x4 (Michael Rock, Donnie Luu). Flat colour blocks, tight grid, one dominant wordmark. Miami / HK / Paris / Tokyo / Seoul.

  • Apothecary tins

    Utilitarian packaging designed to look handsome with wear. The scuffs become part of the object.
