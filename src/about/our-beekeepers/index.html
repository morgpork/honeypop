our beekeepers

The people behind the honey.

Honey Pop works directly with beekeepers across six NZ regions, each managing hives for a different native honey varietal. The honey’s character is what drives the flavour pairing — the balm is built around it, not the other way round. Provenance, for us, is a specific person in a specific place — not a region printed on a label.

the six

The six beekeepers

Image: East Cape beekeeper
East Cape Mānuka honey — pairs with Beeswax

Mānuka honey is the iconic NZ honey: dark, intensely flavoured, high-value. East Cape hives sit in stands of flowering mānuka from December into late summer. Our beekeeper there manages around 400 hives without antibiotics, moves them seasonally, and sells the surplus to us at a fair premium.

Image: Canterbury beekeeper
Canterbury Clover honey — pairs with Vanilla

Clover is the classic NZ table honey: pale gold, mellow, sweet without being sticky. Canterbury’s mid-summer clover fields produce enormous crops. Our Canterbury beekeeper is a second-generation operation — around 600 hives, mostly on local dairy farms.

Image: Westland beekeeper
Westland Kāmahi honey — pairs with Mango

Kāmahi is NZ’s most-exported pale honey — creamy, with a slight caramel note from a West Coast hardwood that flowers in December. Our Westland beekeeper places hives in beech-kāmahi-rimu forest for the flow.

Image: Coromandel beekeeper
Coromandel Pōhutukawa honey — pairs with Honey & Grapefruit

Pōhutukawa honey is light gold with a faint salt from the coastal flowering tree — the ‘NZ Christmas tree’. Our Coromandel beekeeper runs seasonal hives on beachfront land during the pōhutukawa bloom in late December.

Image: Northland beekeeper
Northland Tāwari honey — pairs with Pomegranate

Tāwari is a Northland native with a sharper-edged amber-green honey — fruit-adjacent, not the usual mellow profile. Our Northland beekeeper manages around 200 hives in the Kaipara hills.

Image: West Coast beekeeper
West Coast Rewarewa honey — pairs with Coconut & Pear

Rewarewa is the NZ honeysuckle tree — its honey is copper-toned with caramel notes, denser than clover and more forest-feeling than coastal varietals. Our West Coast beekeeper is a small outfit — 80 hives, all on private forestry land.

All six partnerships are solidifying as the first production batches land. Full beekeeper bios and names will publish here once we can do them justice.
