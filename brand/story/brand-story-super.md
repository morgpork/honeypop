# SUPER — Brand Story

## The Story

A super is the box on top of a beehive, where bees store the honey they don't need. It's where beekeepers take the honey from. We took the name, the wood, and the honey.

SUPER is a wooden matchbox. Inside, an insert. Inside the insert, honey balm — NZ honey, beeswax, coconut oil, jojoba oil. Four ingredients, six flavours. Each lid is its own graphic, designed like the matchbox labels and apothecary tins of the last century, when small everyday things were the best canvas graphic designers had.

You keep the matchbox. When the balm runs out, the insert slides out and a new one slides in. Nothing gets thrown away. The matchbox gets better with handling.

Everything about SUPER is one argument said three ways. The format says keep this. The ingredients say we mean it. The refill says this isn't disposable. Nothing to defend because every part agrees.

Starts with honey balm. Extends to anything that fits in a matchbox.

---

## Audience Truth

"I notice packaging, I read ingredient lists, and I'm tired of everyday stuff being disposable plastic with more marketing than substance."

## Founder Truth

A wooden matchbox, four ingredients of NZ honey, and a graphic worth keeping. Nothing to invent — just the right things put together properly.

## Territory

Small-format craft. Japanese stationery sensibility applied to apothecary goods. Warm, graphic, intentional. Not wellness-reverent, not Gen-Z cheeky, not luxury-cold — obviously well-made.

## The Reframe

HoneyBum was *cheeky name, serious product* — a defence. SUPER is *obvious once you see it* — a demonstration. The packaging, ingredients, and refill model argue for themselves.

## Voice and Tone

### The voice

Warm graphic confidence. Friendly, direct, and fond of the object. Notices design details out loud. Talks about the product with the affection of someone who genuinely likes it. Never wellness-speak, never luxury-cold, never cheeky-for-cheeky.

### Attributes

- **Direct, not cold** — States what it is, what's in it, moves on. Warmth comes from specificity, not adjectives.
- **Fond, not cheeky** — Likes the object. Doesn't need to make a joke to hold attention.
- **Graphic, not decorative** — Every word is doing work. No filler, no ornament.
- **Specific, not abstract** — "NZ honey, in wood" beats "natural ingredients, thoughtfully packaged."

### Word palette

honey, balm, matchbox, bee, beekeeper, super, wood, insert, refill, flavour, graphic, small, nice, proper, made, NZ, four, keep, worth

### Off-limits

- Wellness, clean beauty, ritual, self-care, nourish, hydrate, infused
- Luxe, luxurious, artisan, handcrafted, curated
- Stay buzzin / bee-puns / any juvenile voice
- Any ingredient not actually in the product
- Generic AI filler

### Register

- UK English spelling (flavour, colour, neighbour)
- Contractions fine. The voice is casual but composed.
- "We" for the brand. First-person plural, not third.
- Short sentences preferred. Fragments allowed when they land.

## Brand Line *(v1 — for review)*

**Primary candidate:** Made to keep.
*Two words. Captures the lifecycle / refill argument. Works on packaging, social, investor.*

**Alternate:** Four ingredients. One matchbox.
*Inherits HoneyBum's structural-clarity pattern. Strongest on product-page hero.*

**Alternate:** Obviously.
*Leans on the reframe — "obvious once you see it". High risk, high reward. Works only if the packaging/story already sold you before you read it.*

Both the primary and alternates are first-pass drafts. Expect to iterate in a dedicated tagline round.

## Differentiator

The constraint is the brand. Wooden matchbox + four ingredients + refill insert = one argument made three ways. No competitor in the category has all four (wood packaging, four ingredients, refillable insert, graphic-first design). The matchbox itself is the shelf story in one sentence.

## Product Platform *(v1 — for review)*

SUPER is a matchbox-format goods brand. Every product fits in a pocket-sized wooden matchbox with a refillable insert. The brand scales by format, not by category.

### Launch range: six flavours of honey balm

1. Vanilla
2. Pomegranate
3. Coconut & Pear
4. Beeswax Original
5. Honey & Grapefruit
6. Mango

Carried over from HoneyBum's range. The four-ingredient formula is consistent across all six; the honey varietal and flavour oil change. Each flavour gets its own matchbox-lid graphic.

### Roadmap candidates *(anything that fits the matchbox form)*

- Solid perfume / scent balm
- Multi-use honey salve (hands, cuticles, dry patches)
- Beeswax wrap squares (small format)
- Match packs (literal — on-brand, closes the loop)
- Solid soap or shampoo cubes
- Breath mints / pastilles
- Sewing kit essentials (tiny scissors, thread, needles)
- Incense cones

Every extension uses the same wooden matchbox shell with a format-appropriate insert. The lid graphic signals the category and flavour. No product gets made unless it earns its place in a matchbox.

### Open production questions

- **Wood + oil interaction.** Raw wood absorbs oils and discolours over time. The insert must seal the balm from the wood — options: food-grade lacquered cavity, waxed paper liner, or a drop-in metal/tin cup that holds the balm.
- **Opening mechanism.** Traditional matchbox (slide-out tray) vs. hinged lid vs. magnetic close. Slide-out is most matchbox-authentic.
- **Manufacturing.** NZ wooden matchbox sources at the scale of launch — unknown. Needs supplier research.
- **Insert replacement path.** How a customer buys a refill — direct web, in retail, subscription. Affects pricing and packaging of the insert alone.

These are product questions, not brand questions. Flagged here so they're not lost.

## What Carries Over from HoneyBum

Preserved from the archived HoneyBum brand work:

- Four-ingredient formulation (NZ honey, beeswax, coconut oil, jojoba oil)
- Six launch flavours and the pairings
- NZ honey as hero ingredient
- The positioning-map gap (between $18 wellness ritual and $2 candle wax) — now expressed through format, not price
- Bare Earth / Prada Mode as *imagery and graphic-style* references (not packaging form)

## What Changes from HoneyBum

- **Name:** HoneyBum → SUPER
- **Form factor:** cylindrical plastic tube → wooden matchbox with refillable insert
- **Audience:** Gen Z mass-market impulse → 25–40 design-conscious, keeps nice things
- **Price tier:** ~$7 impulse → ~$25 box + ~$6–10 refill inserts (Aesop/Le Labo refill model)
- **Voice:** "cheeky, not juvenile" → warm graphic confidence
- **Primary reframe:** *cheeky name, serious product* → *obvious once you see it*
- **Brand scope:** lip balm brand → matchbox-format goods brand (starting with lip balm)
