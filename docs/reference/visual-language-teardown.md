# SUPER — Visual Language Teardown

Reference pool for SUPER's wooden-matchbox honey balm identity. Captured 2026-04-15. Not a brief — names the specific works, designers, foundries, and archives to study before drawing a single lid.

Two locked languages:

- **Primary (matchbox lids):** Japanese vintage matchbox labels, 1950s–70s Tokyo printing. Bold single-colour woodblock-feeling marks, flat saturated colour, hand-lettered wordmarks, whimsy without cutesy.
- **Secondary (site display type, posters, campaign):** Prada Mode poster style — big bold type, tight grid, flat colour blocks, constructivist rhythm.

Body prose: serif. Photography (Ghibli-NZ Bare Earth stills) is scoped elsewhere.

---

## 1. Japanese matchbox label archives (primary reference pool)

Study the printing logic — hand-cut, flat inks, one or two colours plus a knockout — as much as the motifs.

**Jane McDevitt (maraid) Flickr set — 418 Japanese matchbox labels, 1920s–40s.** The workhorse visual archive; a private-collection album that launched the modern wave of interest. Flat-colour animals, hand-lettered wordmarks, Meiji geometries colliding with Bauhaus. <https://www.flickr.com/photos/maraid/albums/72157604922299315/>

**Tokyo Metropolitan Library matchbox label collection — 43 scrapbooks.** Canonical institutional holding; catalogued 2022 Bunka Week, indexed commercial/export/advertising. Shows the commercial logic: small frame, single colour, wordmark on top. <https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/english/bunkaweek/2022/matchlabel/>

**Ryohei Kojima (1935–) — Light Publicity, Tokyo.** Matchbox aesthetic scaled up into the blue-chip mid-century poster. Kojima deconstructs subjects into their simplest flat-colour shapes — the distillation move a SUPER lid needs. V&A-collected from 1968 onwards. <https://grainedit.com/2011/02/15/ryohei-kojima/>

**Tadanori Yokoo — "Morning Sun" rayed poster series, 1965.** Reappropriated 1930s matchbox-cover motifs as pop-constructivist fuel. Load-bearing: reading a matchbox label as flat iconography, not nostalgia. Friedman Benda catalogued the 1965–71 silkscreens. <https://www.friedmanbenda.com/exhibitions/tadanori-yokoo-the-aesthetics-of-end-early-silkscreens-from-1965-1971/>

**Shin Matsunaga — Shiseido Bronze Summer, 1969–71.** Three consecutive Tokyo ADC prizes. Clean typography, one saturated colour block, wordmark doing the work. Bridge between the matchbox and Prada Mode lanes. <https://a-g-i.org/user/shinmatsunaga/>

**"The Art of Japanese Matchbox Labels" (Shimojima Masao, intro by Tadanori Yokoo).** Book-form canon, organised by subject — flowers, fruit, animals, scenery, lettering, transportation. Single physical object for the studio desk. <https://www.bakezoribooks.com/shop/p/acd4feeyxoejzil2o2oxhih6lryty2>

**Matchbox Label Collection 1920s–40s (Pie Books, ISBN 4-89444-357-0).** Japanese-language compendium. Reference for hand-drawn wordmark inside decorative cartouche — the exact move SUPER's lids pull. <https://www.ebay.com/p/14031417929>

**Yōkai Senjafuda matchbox label series (University of Oregon, Mellon Projects).** Academic archive of spirit/creature labels. How designers built characterful single-subject marks inside a ~35×55mm frame — SUPER's exact constraint. <https://glam.uoregon.edu/yokaisenjafuda/page/matchbox-label-series>

Gap: could not verify matchbox-specific bodies of work by Toshihiro Katayama or Maira Kalman in reputable sources. Treat as not-a-reference unless the designer surfaces a primary.

## 2. Prada Mode poster references

Prada Mode is Prada Group's itinerant private art club. Identity by **2x4 (Michael Rock, Donnie Luu)** — a single flexible system that re-skins per edition. Big tight type, colour-blocked, constructivist.

**Prada Mode Miami (2018)** — inaugural, Freehand Miami, Art Basel. Set the tone: huge lockup, flat colour block, minimal secondary type. <https://www.prada.com/us/en/pradasphere/events.html>

**Prada Mode Hong Kong (2019)** — Tai Kwun, Art Basel HK. "Prada Invites" and Jamie Diamond's Dolls' House. Architectural grid applied to a hospitality context. <https://www.prada.com/eu/en/pradasphere/events/2019/prada-mode-hong-kong.html>

**Prada Mode Paris (2020)** — Maxim's, curated by Kate Crawford and Trevor Paglen. Palette shifts to the venue; the typographic system holds. Proof the language is a chassis, not a style. <https://us.fashionnetwork.com/news/Prada-mode-club-to-stage-paris-session-during-haute-couture-week,1170425.html>

**Prada Mode Tokyo (May 2023)** — Teien Art Museum, curated by Kazuyo Sejima. Japanese architectural restraint on the same grid — the edition that most directly bridges SUPER's two languages. <https://www.designboom.com/art/kazuyo-sejima-prada-mode-tokyo-teien-art-museum-05-12-2023/>

**Prada Mode Seoul (September 2023), "Plural and Parallel"** — KOTE, Frieze Seoul. 10th edition. Tight bilingual lockup (Korean + Latin) — reference for SUPER's flavour-name + wordmark stacking. <https://www.pradagroup.com/en/perspectives/stories/sezione-progetti-speciali/prada-mode-seoul.html>

**Consistent kit across editions** (per 2x4's project page): one dominant wordmark, colour-block panel behind or beside type, location/date as secondary meta, tight grid, no decoration. Flat panels, big type, hard edges. <https://2x4.org/work/prada-mode/>

## 3. Parallel references worth knowing

Adjacent pools. Pull one move from each, not a whole vocabulary.

**Bureau Mirko Borsche (Munich).** Bayerische Staatsoper identity — black-and-white posters, opera reduced to a type lockup. BR Symphonieorchester (2010–) — tight lockup, white-on-black, stage contrast. For SUPER poster/campaign rhythm. <https://bureauborsche.com/projects>

**Paula Scher / Pentagram — The Public Theater (1994–).** American wood-type on theatre advertising. Unorthodox spacing, mixed weights, loud urban voice. Canonical "the typography is the poster" — for when Latin type carries a whole SUPER campaign alone. <https://www.pentagram.com/work/the-public-theater-2020-2021-season>

**Wim Crouwel — "Vormgevers" poster, Stedelijk 1967.** Grid visible on the surface; typeface drawn into the grid. Mr. Gridnik. For poster-mode: the grid is the design, not the container. <https://www.stedelijk.nl/en/exhibitions/wim-crouwel>

**Early ICA London / Design Council archive.** British post-war ephemera held at Brighton Design Archives. Cooler, drier counterpoint to the Japanese warmth; useful for information hierarchy. <https://archiveshub.jisc.ac.uk/search/archives/4398c1f7-cacd-3c33-8c90-2791e7fb7c66>

**Cooper Black / mid-century Latin letterforms.** Oswald Bruce Cooper, 1922 — ultrabold serif powering 60s–70s packaging and album covers (Pet Sounds, 1966). For a SUPER wordmark that wants warmth-and-swagger over austerity. The other door, not the default. <https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/7357/cooper-black>

## 4. Typography candidates for SUPER

Specific typefaces. Each with foundry, best-use context, rough licensing note.

### Display (wordmark + H1–H2)

- **GT Walsheim Poster** — Grilli Type, Noël Leu (2010). Inspired by Otto Baumberger's 1930s Swiss poster lettering. Idiosyncratic G, generous R, warm-but-precise. Commercial, ~CHF 300 full family; tiered web. <https://www.grillitype.com/typeface/gt-walsheim>
- **Standard CT** — CastleType, Jason Castle (1991). Digitised from the Swiss Standard family, 1950s. Warmer and more idiosyncratic than Helvetica/Akzidenz — right temperature for SUPER. Commercial, ~USD 40 per weight. <https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/574/standard-ct>
- **Inter Display** — Rasmus Andersson (2016–). Free, SIL OFL. Neo-grotesque with a true display optical size; 9 weights + italics. Sensible open-source default. <https://rsms.me/inter/>
- **Space Grotesk** — Florian Karsten (2018, SIL OFL), derived from Space Mono. Quirky display grotesque with monospace DNA — pairs natively with a mono data chip. For a slightly odd wordmark. <https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Grotesk>

### Body (prose — long-form only)

- **Tiempos Text** — Klim Type Foundry, Kris Sowersby (NZ). Modern transitional serif built from Plantin/Times for editorial/newsprint. NZ provenance aligns. Commercial, from NZD ~550 per weight desktop. <https://klim.co.nz/fonts/tiempos-text/>
- **Freight Text Pro** — Joshua Darden, GarageFonts (2005). 18th-century Dutch warmth, workhorse. Twelve styles; on Adobe Fonts with CC. <https://fonts.adobe.com/fonts/freight-text>
- **Newsreader** — Production Type for Google Fonts. Free, SIL OFL. Built for on-screen long-form; 3 optical sizes, 7 weights, 42 styles. Open-source default. <https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Newsreader>

### Mono (data chips, ingredient meta, batch numbers)

- **GT Cinetype Mono** — Grilli Type (2015), from cinema subtitling laser machinery. Warm mechanical — not sterile developer mono. Commercial. <https://www.grillitype.com/typeface/gt-cinetype>
- **Space Mono** — Colophon Foundry via Google Fonts. Free, SIL OFL. Matches Space Grotesk. Zero-budget answer. <https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Mono>

## 5. Colour direction

Concrete starting palette for SUPER. Every value reads as saturated-warm, never pastel, never corporate. Hex values are starting points for the designer's paint-chip pass.

| Role | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Paper background | `#f7efe0` | Warmer than Ffern's `#fcfbf7` sand. Reads as uncoated kraft-adjacent — the matchbox paper itself. |
| Anchor dark | `#1a1614` | Near-black with a brown undertone. Higher contrast than Ffern's terracotta; holds poster-mode type without looking corporate. |
| Honey / gold accent | `#d89a2a` | Saturated amber. The "super" accent — CTAs, flash callouts, packaging foil if it ever goes physical. |
| Hairline / border | `#d7c9ac` | 4-shade desaturation of the paper. Sits quiet under type. |

### Six per-flavour accents (matchbox lid dominants)

Each chosen to be woodblock-legal: one hue, flat, saturated, printable as a single-plate ink.

| Flavour | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Vanilla | `#e6b56a` | Toasted custard gold — warmer than cream, paler than the honey accent. Distinguishes Vanilla from Beeswax Original. |
| Pomegranate | `#a81d2e` | Saturated arterial red. 1960s Japanese matchbox red exactly. |
| Coconut & Pear | `#6e8e3e` | Saturated mossy green — pear skin, not mint. Reads as produce not toothpaste. |
| Beeswax Original | `#c68418` | Burnt amber. The hero/original colour — deeper than the honey accent so the two don't collide. |
| Honey & Grapefruit | `#e4622a` | Citrus red-orange. Sits between amber and pomegranate; holds its own on the paper. |
| Mango | `#e89420` | Saturated fruit orange. Warmer than honey, brighter than beeswax. |

Two rules for the palette system: (1) no two adjacent flavours in the grid share a hue family; (2) every per-flavour accent must pass WCAG AA against the anchor dark at 18px+.

## 6. Matchbox lid design principles

Five rules. Any future lid must pass all five.

1. **One subject, one dominant colour, one wordmark.** No secondary illustration, no subtitle. The lid works at 35×55mm because it is a single legible mark.
2. **Flat colour only.** No gradient, no photograph, no faux-depth texture. One or two flat inks plus a knockout. If it can't print on a Risograph, it's not a SUPER lid.
3. **Warm-saturated palette, never pastel.** The Japanese 1950s–70s palette is saturated and warm by default. Pastel reads as Western craft-market. Every flavour accent is a real colour, not a tint.
4. **SUPER wordmark in a locked position across all flavours.** Top-center or top-left — pick one in the first lid and never move it. Consistency of position turns six lids into one brand.
5. **The flavour mark is hand-drawn, not typeset.** The system typeface handles everything else (box sides, site, posters). The flavour name on the lid is always a custom-drawn mark — a deliberate break from the grid. That's the whimsy.
