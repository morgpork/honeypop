# HoneyBum — Brand Teardowns

Competitive research and inspiration references.

---

## Activist Manuka (activistmanuka.com)

**What they are:** Ultra-premium NZ manuka honey skincare. Founded by Gabrielle (ex-Vogue/Harper's Bazaar art director) and Luke (surfer, beekeeper). US-based, NZ-sourced.

**Positioning:** Luxury. Lip balm $35 USD. Honey mask $75. Raw manuka honey $95.

**What works:**
- Provenance is the product — every ingredient traced to a specific NZ region
- Less is more philosophy — small range (~12 products), explicitly anti-excessive-consumerism
- Founder story is authentic — surfer + art director, earned credibility
- "Objects" and "Collectibles" as product categories — product IS the design object
- Sustainability reads like a personal letter, not corporate policy

**What HoneyBum takes:**
- Fewer products done right is a brand position
- NZ provenance as ingredient story, not flag-waving
- Product as object (their ceramic vessel = our colour-stacked tube)

**What HoneyBum avoids:**
- The $35 price point (different universe)
- The reverent, serious tone
- Wellness/clinical language

## Kobee's Co (kobeesco.com)

**What they are:** $5 handmade lip balms, Gen Z branding, bee/sustainability angle. US-based.

**What works:**
- $5 price point — impulse buy territory
- Flavour range is fun — watermelon, passion fruit, pineapple, mango, peach, strawberry, vanilla
- The Hive Project — personally install beehives, plant trees, clean ocean plastic. Documented.
- Product range is focused — lip balms, lip scrubs, hand salve, lotion sticks

**What's weak:**
- No philosophy underneath the fun exterior. "Stay buzzin buddy" is a slogan, not a belief.
- Ingredients are NOT simple — 5+ per tube, 20+ across range
- "Our Story" page is literally empty — no text
- Merch (t-shirts, sweatshirts) feels scattered
- Design is generic Shopify — nothing distinctive

**What HoneyBum takes:**
- $5–7 is the right price range
- Flavour bundles/packs drive gifting and collection
- The gap: beautiful design + real philosophy. Kobee's has neither. HoneyBum has both.

## Bare Earth (Behance — Yuliia Hrabynska)

**What it is:** AI-rendered body care brand concept. 25K appreciations. Featured in Behance Packaging.

**What makes it work:**
- The renders ARE the brand — hyper-lush Ghibli-meets-real landscapes
- Colour as environment — each product's palette extends into its entire world
- Scalloped/cloud-shaped label is the signature shape across all SKUs
- Typography: retro script for product names + chunky block serif for brand name
- Product as monolith — oversized in frame, dominates the scene

**What HoneyBum takes:**
- AI-rendered scene approach — each flavour gets its own NZ landscape world
- Each flavour = its own colour world
- Product as monolith composition — tube dominates the scene
- The stacked colour bands are HoneyBum's signature (where Bare Earth has cloud labels)

**What HoneyBum avoids:**
- Retro script typography (not HoneyBum's vibe)
- Cloud/scallop label shape
- Overall sweetness and dreaminess — HoneyBum is cheeky and direct

## Burt's Bees (the gap)

**What happened:** Started as a real person (Burt Shavitz) making products from beeswax in Maine. Had soul, character, a weird bearded guy on the label. Clorox bought them. Now it's a corporate SKU factory with 50+ products and no connection to the original story.

**What they get wrong now:**
- Too many flavours and products — diluted identity
- Lost the "Burt" — the character, the soul, the simplicity
- Feels like a big corporate brand because it is
- Packaging isn't boring but lacks the cheeky personality

**The gap HoneyBum fills:**
- Pick up what Burt's dropped — the soul, the simplicity, the character
- Four ingredients (Burt's has 10+)
- Six flavours (Burt's has 15+)
- Design-forward packaging (Burt's looks dated)
- Cheeky brand voice (Burt's is earnest/corporate)
